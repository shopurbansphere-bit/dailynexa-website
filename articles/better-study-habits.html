How to Build Better Study Habits

Sarah Jenkins
Education • 7 min read • ↓ SCROLL DOWN FOR KEY

Good study habits are more important than long hours. Here are simple methods that actually work.

1. Use the Pomodoro technique

Study for 25 minutes, then take a 5-minute break. After four rounds, take a longer break. This keeps your mind fresh and reduces burnout.

2. Active recall

Instead of only reading notes, close the book and try to remember what you just learned. Testing yourself is one of the most effective ways to strengthen memory.

3. Spaced repetition

Review material after one day, then after three days, then after a week. Spreading out your revision helps information stay in long-term memory.

4. Remove distractions

Put your phone in another room or use focus apps. Even small notifications can break concentration for several minutes.

5. Teach what you learn

Explain the topic out loud as if you are teaching a friend. If you can explain it simply, you understand it well.

Start with one or two of these habits and build from there.
