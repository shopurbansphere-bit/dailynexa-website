How Wi-Fi Works

Technology · 5 min read

Wi-Fi lets your phone, laptop and other devices connect to the internet without cables. Here is how it works in simple terms.

The basic idea

Your router is connected to the internet through a cable from your internet provider. The router then sends and receives data using radio waves. Your devices pick up those radio waves and communicate back.

Radio frequencies

Most home Wi-Fi uses the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands. 2.4 GHz travels farther but is slower and more crowded. 5 GHz is faster but has shorter range.

SSID and password

The network name you see (SSID) identifies your Wi-Fi. The password encrypts the connection so only authorized devices can join.

Why speed varies

Distance from the router, walls, other networks nearby, and the number of connected devices all affect your speed.

Understanding these basics helps you place your router better and choose the right settings for stronger Wi-Fi.
